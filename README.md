Product Hunt is still the best-known place to launch, but it is no longer the only one, and for most makers it isn't the easiest. A Product Hunt launch lasts 24 hours, rewards whoever can rally the biggest audience in the first few hours, and its links are nofollow. Launching on several alternatives spreads your launch over weeks, reaches audiences Product Hunt doesn't, and earns lasting backlinks along the way.

This list is curated by hand. Every entry says **what kind of platform it is, how a launch or listing works, and who it suits best**, so you can pick the right places instead of submitting everywhere.

## 🚀 Launch platforms

Closest to the Product Hunt experience: a launch page, votes and a ranking, but with different formats, audiences and rules.

### [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com)
The original. Each day's launches go live at 12:01 a.m. Pacific time and compete for upvotes and comments for 24 hours, and the best are featured on the homepage and in the daily newsletter. Launching is free, but links are nofollow, so its value is the audience and the badge rather than SEO.
**Best for:** products with a community ready to show up on launch day.

### [Uneed](https://www.uneed.best)
A launch platform and directory for makers with daily launches and a ranked list. It is free to submit, or you can pay to skip the line, and listings get a dofollow backlink. It also runs a private Discord community.
**Best for:** indie products that want a launch plus a lasting directory listing.

### [Noonlaunch](https://noonlaunch.com)
A weekly launch directory where products collect votes during their launch week, winners go into a hall of fame, and every product keeps a permanent page. Free launches wait in a reviewed queue and the top three each week get a dofollow link; a low-cost Premium launch skips the queue and always gets one.
**Best for:** indie makers and small SaaS teams on a budget.

### [ufind.best](https://ufind.best)
Launches are grouped into weekly cohorts and ranked by community votes together with an AI jury that scores the product itself rather than the maker's following. Free launches need its badge, and the top three free launches each week earn a lifetime dofollow link.
**Best for:** tools with a clear, demo-able product.

### [Startup Fast](https://www.startupfa.st)
Daily launches for startups, with each day's batch featured on the homepage. Free launches join a queue and the day's top three with its badge get a dofollow link; a paid launch skips the queue.
**Best for:** early startups that want a quick, low-cost launch day.

### [Fazier](https://fazier.com)
A launch platform for makers and startups with launch pages and rankings.
**Best for:** a second launch after your main one.

### [MicroLaunch](https://microlaunch.net)
Gives products a longer launch window than a single day, so feedback and votes build up over time.
**Best for:** makers who prefer a slow build-up to a one-day sprint.

### [Peerlist Launchpad](https://peerlist.io/launchpad)
Weekly launches inside Peerlist, a professional network popular with developers and designers, tied to your profile.
**Best for:** products made for builders.

### [TinyLaunch](https://www.tinylaunch.com)
A launch platform aimed at small and indie products.
**Best for:** side projects and small SaaS tools.

## 🧪 Pre-launch and beta

**Best for:** waitlists and products still in beta.

### [Launching Next](https://www.launchingnext.com)

## 🛠 Developer tools

### [DevHunt](https://devhunt.org)
A launch platform for developer tools, voted on by developers, with weekly launches.
**Best for:** APIs, libraries, CLIs and dev tooling.

### [DEV Community](https://dev.to)
Not a launch site, but a write-up of how you built your tool, or the problem it solves, reaches a large developer audience.
**Best for:** developer tools and technical stories.

## 💬 Communities

No leaderboard, but often better conversations and feedback. Read each community's rules before you post.

### [Hacker News: Show HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/show)
Share something people can try right now with a technical, critical audience. Use a plain title, skip the marketing language, and be ready to answer questions in the comments.
**Best for:** technical products and honest feedback.

### [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com)
A community of bootstrapped founders. Share your story, numbers and lessons, not just a link.
**Best for:** building in public and founder-to-founder feedback.

### [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/SideProject/)
r/SideProject, r/SaaS, r/startups and the niche subreddits where your users already spend time. Self-promotion rules vary, so contribute first and share second.
**Best for:** reaching a specific niche directly.

## 📇 Startup and tool directories

Permanent listings that keep bringing visitors, and often a backlink, long after launch day.

### [Startup Fame](https://startupfa.me)
A curated startup directory where every listing is reviewed by a person, organised by category, location and founder. The free plan needs its badge on your homepage; paid plans skip the badge and go live immediately. Listings carry a dofollow link.
**Best for:** a reviewed, permanent startup profile.

### [twelve.tools](https://twelve.tools)
Features twelve new tools every day, picked from submissions and tagged by category. The free plan gives a dofollow link in return for a link back; a one-time Pro plan gives more permanent links and faster listing.
**Best for:** indie tools that want a dofollow link from a high-authority site.

### [Findly.tools](https://findly.tools)
A large, human-reviewed directory of SaaS products and tools. The free listing keeps a dofollow link while its badge stays on your site; a one-time Premium plan needs no badge.
**Best for:** any SaaS product or tool that wants a permanent listing.

### [SideProjectors](https://www.sideprojectors.com)
Showcase side projects, and even sell the ones you no longer want to run.
**Best for:** side projects looking for users or a buyer.

### [Launch Llama](https://tools.launchllama.co)
A directory of AI tools and startups where free listings stay up for good after review, and some are picked for its newsletters, which reach 69,000+ subscribers. A paid Featured listing skips the queue and goes live the same day.
**Best for:** AI tools and SaaS products that want a lasting listing and a chance at newsletter exposure.

## 🤖 AI tools directories

People searching for "an AI tool for…" land on these directories' category and comparison pages.

### [There's An AI For That](https://theresanaiforthat.com)
One of the largest AI tools directories, organised by task.
**Best for:** consumer and prosumer AI products.

### [Futurepedia](https://www.futurepedia.io)
A large, frequently updated AI tools directory with categories and guides.
**Best for:** AI tools with a clear use case.

### [Submit AI Tools](https://submitaitools.org)
An AI tools directory with more than 10,000 tools organised by category, with alternatives for each. Listing a tool is free; featured spots are sold separately.
**Best for:** showing up in "alternatives to" comparisons.

### [Good AI Tools](https://goodaitools.com)
A directory of AI software for professionals and creators. Every submission goes through an editorial review, and it says it doesn't sell pay-for-placement listings.
**Best for:** a reviewed listing next to similar tools.

### [Dofollow.Tools](https://dofollow.tools)
A directory of AI tools and SaaS products with dofollow links: free listings wait in a queue and need its badge, paid listings skip both.
**Best for:** AI and SaaS products whose main goal is a backlink.

## 📚 Software directories and review sites

Slower to build up, but they put you in front of buyers actively comparing software.

### [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net)
List your product as an alternative to well-known tools, where people are already looking for something different.
**Best for:** products that replace a popular tool.

### [SaaSHub](https://www.saashub.com)
Software discovery and alternatives, useful for long-term visibility.
**Best for:** SaaS products in established categories.

### [G2](https://www.g2.com) and [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com)
The big B2B review sites. A free profile plus a handful of real customer reviews builds trust with buyers.
**Best for:** B2B SaaS with paying customers.

## How to choose where to launch

1. **Go where your users are.** Developer tools belong on DevHunt and Show HN; AI tools on AI directories; B2B SaaS on review sites. A smaller, relevant audience beats a big, general one.
2. **Pick a format you can win.** Without an existing audience, a week-long launch like [LaunchStall](https://launchstall.com) is far easier than a 24-hour sprint on Product Hunt.
3. **Check the link before you count on it.** Many platforms give a dofollow link only to their top three, to paid launches, or while their badge is on your site.
4. **Read the fine print on badges.** Badge-based free plans are great value, but the listing (or the link) usually disappears if you remove the badge.
5. **Space out your launches.** One launch a week for a month keeps traffic coming in and gives you time to fix what each launch teaches you.

## FAQ

**What is the best alternative to Product Hunt?**
For most makers, [LaunchStall](https://launchstall.com): a full week to collect votes instead of 24 hours, ten launches per edition so you aren't buried, a permanent product page and a dofollow backlink, and it's free with a badge.

**Is it worth launching on Product Hunt in 2026?**
It can be, if you already have an audience to bring on launch day. Otherwise, launch on a few alternatives first and use what you learn to make your Product Hunt launch stronger.

**Are Product Hunt alternatives free?**
Most have a free option, sometimes with a review queue or a badge on your site. Paid plans usually skip the wait, guarantee a backlink or give more prominent placement.

**Do these platforms help SEO?**
The ones that give a dofollow link from an established site can lift your Domain Rating, and their listing pages often rank for your brand name. Product Hunt's own links are nofollow.

**How many launch platforms should I use?**
Five to ten relevant ones over a few weeks is a good start. Relevance matters more than volume.

**Can I launch on several platforms at once?**
Yes, but spreading launches over a few weeks gives each one your full attention and keeps traffic steady.
